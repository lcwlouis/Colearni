# Product brief

## Promise

Desk is a calm place to work through difficult ideas and build understanding the learner can use without the application. AI supports the work; generated output is not the product's purpose.

The founder's problem hypotheses are overwhelming subjects with no starting point, attention lost during passive or verbose instruction, and readily available answers mistaken for learning. These are grounded in the founder's experience; they are not established findings about every potential customer.

## Product model

A **desk** organises a learning intention that can span sources and sessions. A paper can start a desk, but does not define its limits. A **session** is one period of work. The **working surface** holds current activity and limited support; a **shelf** holds saved sources and activities without demanding attention. Notes also belong to a reusable personal knowledge collection.

The learner may arrive with a broad goal, paper, article, problem or code. The system can research material when none is supplied. Research extends a useful path rather than generating an overwhelming curriculum first.

## Core experience

Bring an intention or source → choose a capability in plain language → optionally establish a starting point → work on one manageable activity → obtain situated support → revise or apply → stop at a meaningful boundary → return with a small retrieval or application opportunity.

The source remains reachable. Background sources, author claims, learner writing, and tutor explanations remain distinguishable. Reading, note-writing, exploration and practice can coexist without forcing a panel switch that loses the learner's work.

## Teaching principles

- Help is available without being earned. A beginner does not need to ask the perfect question.
- Explain one coherent difficulty at a time, without long default essays or mandatory sentence-by-sentence continuation clicks.
- Invite meaningful activity rather than adding an examination after every explanation.
- Increase support when stuck; do not repeatedly ask the same question. Direct solutions may be revealed explicitly, with assistance recorded rather than moralised.
- Adapt to prior understanding, task, preferences and accessibility. Do not classify people into permanent learning-style identities.
- Bloom categories support planning; ask users what they want to be able to do, not which taxonomy level they want.
- A successful session can end without a graded assessment. Unknown understanding is not failure.
- Graphs orient and connect knowledge; they do not gate explanations or automatically transfer mastery.
- Learner notes are a place to think imperfectly. No unsolicited editing, continuous grading or default AI autocomplete.

## Initial audience and scope

**Proposed pilot, not a validated market:** adult self-directed learners and students who want to understand technical material. Use a synthetic paper-like affine-function scenario for interface tests; recruit a real audience and select reviewed real material before the learning pilot. Public schools and universities are in the access vision, but a minors-facing school launch requires separate privacy and safeguarding work.

First frontend demonstration: source passage, learner note, worked example, worksheet response, a simple reviewed simulation, a research result on the shelf, separate tutor contribution, selective undo and a return state. No model or parsing claims are implied by this fixture.

First real learning slice: a source-anchored intention with secure saved work, scoped tutoring, modest research and independently reviewable learning evidence. Avoid feature-parity rebuilding of the old dashboard and graph.

## Access and sustainability

A capable self-hosted edition with user-controlled model connections and portable data is part of the intended product. A paid managed service is a hypothesis for funding convenience, infrastructure, support and continued development. Free software use does not imply free inference, research or hardware. Final licensing and pricing remain open; see LICENSING_BUSINESS.md.

## Not launch requirements

An open plugin marketplace, arbitrary generated engines, simultaneous human multiplayer, a universal deduplicated world graph, offline-first sync, full school administration, clinical teaching use, universal course coverage, or unlimited model usage. Keep extension boundaries without implementing speculative platforms.

## Success criteria

Observe whether a learner finds a start, stays meaningfully involved, requests useful help, can explain or apply something independently, and returns without losing their place. Measure delay, friction, errors, cost and perceived control. Do not optimise primarily for message count, card count or time spent.
