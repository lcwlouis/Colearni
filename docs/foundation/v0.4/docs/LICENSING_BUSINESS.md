# Licensing intent and business hypothesis

**Not an operative licence or legal advice. No licence has been selected or applied by this package.** Obtain qualified software-licensing review before publication or accepting contributions under new terms.

## Founder intent

Permit personal learning, public educational deployment and internal workplace learning without a software licence fee. Provide a useful self-hosted application with user-controlled infrastructure/model connections and data portability. The founder is open to revenue from managed hosting, support and commercial partnerships. Exact commercial redistribution restrictions and legal form remain provisional.

## Options and tradeoffs

A licence restricting commercial use is source-available, not open source under the OSI definition. [L1]

MIT permits copying, modification, redistribution and sale with its notice conditions; choosing it would allow competing proprietary offerings rather than reserve commercial rights. [L2]

The current leading proposal is source-available terms that distinguish internal learning from reselling or providing Desk itself as a paid product/service. It is not settled merely because it appeared in a mockup. AGPL remains an alternative for reciprocity rather than resale prohibition; its detailed obligations need review before selection, and were not re-analysed for this pack.

Review existing code, historical grants, contributor rights and dependency licences. A publicly readable repository is not automatically unrestricted. Choose contributor terms deliberately instead of assuming the founder can later relicense every contribution. General licensing guidance warns about the cost of nonstandard terms and ownership/dependency issues. [L3]

## Cases counsel must resolve

A university charging tuition; mixed public/private research; a contractor maintaining a school's server; a paid teacher using Desk during teaching; an employer's internal learning; a vendor reselling a white-label service; a third-party engine developer; transfer of a school deployment; and redistribution of modifications. Selling education should not silently become equivalent to selling Desk.

Keep application-code terms separate from third-party plugin terms, uploaded materials, personal notes and public learning-pack content. Publicly funded institutions do not automatically own rights to redistribute every source they use.

## Business hypothesis

Free self-hosting offers the core learning loop, essential safety and export. Paid hosting provides setup, maintenance, reliable storage, managed inference/research, upgrades and support. Institutional services can fund deployment/operations without restricting learners' right to self-host. Commercial embedding/resale terms may become another revenue source.

Do not weaken the free edition to manufacture inconvenience. Do not promise unlimited model/research usage before measuring costs. Calculate contribution margin after inference, search, storage, payments and support. Test willingness to pay through pilots; educational potential is not proof of demand or sustainable unit economics.

## Launch gates

Final rights holder, licence text, dependency/contributor audit, privacy/terms, trademark/name review, price and provider-spend policy, and applicable school rules remain open. Do not publish an MIT/AGPL/custom licence or label the product open source on the founder's behalf.
